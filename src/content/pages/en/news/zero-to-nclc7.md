“How long will it take?” is a reasonable question when French has to fit around work and family. A useful answer separates scheduled teaching, independent practice and demonstrated readiness.

> **Hours are a planning estimate. NCLC 7 is a test result in four abilities, not a certificate automatically earned after attending a course.**

## B2 and NCLC 7 are not interchangeable labels

The CEFR describes language performance through levels such as B1 and B2. The NCLC framework describes Canadian French-language benchmarks. They help discuss progress, but an approximate relationship is not a substitute for the official results and conversions required by an application.

The CEFR itself does not prescribe a universal 500-hour journey from zero to B2. Starting language background, previous learning, intensity, feedback and practice conditions all affect the time needed.

## Understand what a course timetable includes

Global French Education’s full pathway is organized around 330 scheduled hours and an indicative period of about 14 months. That timetable describes a course pathway. It does not guarantee a particular examination result within that period.

| Part of the plan | What it provides |
|---|---|
| Classes | Explanation, guided practice and feedback. |
| Independent study | Retrieval, listening, reading and preparation. |
| Speaking and writing practice | Opportunities to produce language and revise it. |
| Assessments | Evidence for adjusting the next stage. |

Ask how a proposed timetable accounts for holidays, missed classes and weaker skills. A plan with no room for revision is likely to be fragile.

## Track outcomes at each stage

At the beginner stage, look for clear basic communication. Later, check whether you can follow connected speech, explain an opinion and write a coherent response without copying a model. Before an examination, practise unfamiliar tasks under the correct conditions.

If one skill lags, give it specific attention rather than assuming overall attendance will resolve the gap. Retesting readiness is more useful than defending an original deadline.

The best first estimate follows an assessment of your starting point and an honest weekly schedule. Keep the estimate open to review as evidence of progress accumulates.
