You recognize the topic, catch several familiar words and feel that you understand. Then the answer key disagrees. Often the missing piece is a small distinction: who changed a plan, whether an event is confirmed, or which condition applies.

> **Separate what you heard from what you inferred. Precision begins when you can identify the evidence for your answer.**

## Give the first listen a clear purpose

Choose a short recording appropriate to your level. After one play, note the speaker, situation, main message and one important detail. Add a question mark beside anything uncertain.

This prevents a familiar topic from filling gaps in your understanding. Hearing “train,” “tomorrow” and “late” does not establish whether a train is delayed, a booking was changed or someone plans to leave later.

## Check the cause of the gap

Listen again, then consult the transcript. Mark the phrase that proves or contradicts your interpretation. Classify the problem: unknown vocabulary, an unfamiliar sound connection, a missed negative or an assumption made too early.

| Difficulty | A focused response |
|---|---|
| You knew the word on paper | Practise recognizing it inside a short spoken phrase. |
| You missed a condition | Note words such as *si*, *sauf* and *à condition que*. |
| You confused speakers | Track who proposes, accepts or objects. |
| You guessed from the topic | Require one supporting phrase for each conclusion. |

## Finish by explaining the message

Give a short summary in simple French. Do not copy the transcript. If a detail remains uncertain, say so rather than inventing it. Then listen to a new recording on a related topic to check transfer.

Fifteen focused minutes can produce a useful practice session, but no number of days guarantees a particular jump in level. Track whether repeated error types become less frequent. Keep some enjoyable, less analytical listening as well: sustained attention and precise review serve complementary purposes.
