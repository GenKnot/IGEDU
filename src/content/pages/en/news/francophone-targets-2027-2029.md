Canada’s 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan sets a rising share of French-speaking permanent resident admissions outside Quebec. These targets describe future policy intentions, rather than completed results or guaranteed places for individual applicants.

> **Read targets as targets:** these figures describe intended future admissions outside Quebec, not a guaranteed selection schedule.

## What the plan sets out

| Year | Francophone admissions target outside Quebec | Planned number in the plan |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2027 | 9.5% | 31,825 |
| 2028 | 10.5% | 35,175 |
| 2029 | 12% | 40,200 |

These are outside-Quebec targets. They are not percentages of invitations in the French-language Express Entry category, and the planned numbers should not be read as places reserved for one examination or course provider.

## A planning horizon is not a countdown

A multi-year target provides context for someone considering a sustained learning project. It does not establish a deadline by which “French stops working.” Nor does it guarantee that category definitions, provincial priorities or scoring rules will remain unchanged throughout the period.

Admissions also occur later than many selection decisions. A person admitted in one year may have received an invitation and submitted an application earlier. Comparing a target for admissions with a same-year invitation total therefore mixes two timelines.

## Use the policy context constructively

For a beginner, the useful question is whether there is time and capacity to develop the required skills while maintaining the rest of the project. For someone already approaching the required level, the priorities may be assessment, test validity and document readiness.

In both cases, separate language learning from eligibility advice. A school can help establish a learning plan and evaluate progress. It cannot reserve immigration places, promise policy continuity or confirm eligibility for every pathway.

Review the plan as new official information appears. Treat its direction as context for preparation, while keeping decisions flexible enough to respond to changes in your circumstances and in the rules.
