Running out of time on TCF Canada is not always a reading-speed problem. It can also come from spending too long on one uncertain answer or carrying an unfinished task into the next one.

> **Practise the timing of each section. A single “seconds per question” rule does not fit the whole examination.**

## Know the current structure

| Section | Format | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Listening | 39 multiple-choice questions | 35 minutes |
| Reading | 39 multiple-choice questions | 60 minutes |
| Writing | Three tasks | 60 minutes |
| Speaking | Three tasks | 12 minutes, including two minutes of preparation |

Check the provider’s current instructions before your session. The speaking preparation time belongs to a specific task; it should not be assumed to precede every response.

## Find where time disappears

During one practice session, note when you hesitate, reread unnecessarily or rewrite sentences repeatedly. The goal is to identify a pattern. If unknown vocabulary causes most delays, more timed tests alone will not build the missing vocabulary.

For reading, distinguish information needed to answer the question from details you merely want to understand. For writing, sketch the purpose and structure before drafting. A short plan can prevent a complete restart halfway through a response.

## Alternate training and simulation

Untimed work lets you analyse why an answer is supported and why another option is misleading. Timed work tests whether you can apply that understanding under the examination’s constraints. Both are necessary; they answer different questions.

Use unfamiliar material for simulations. Respect the official instructions for audio playback and the navigation rules explained by your centre. Do not build a strategy around controls that may be unavailable in your test format.

Afterwards, review errors by cause: misunderstood language, missed detail, misread instruction or time management. Work on the largest recurring cause before attempting another full simulation. Sustainable pace comes from understanding and decision-making, not from rushing every item.
