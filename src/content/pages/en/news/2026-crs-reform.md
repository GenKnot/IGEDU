The Express Entry scoring changes effective November 3, 2026 introduce a Labour Market Integration factor. French-speaking candidates need to review the whole profile, because category-based selection still uses CRS ranking.

> **The central rule:** the new factor compares eligible Canadian work experience with a qualifying high-wage job offer and uses the higher result. The two options are not added together.

## Read the maximum correctly

| Option within the new factor | Maximum |
|---|---:|
| Canadian experience, without an accompanying spouse or partner | 150 |
| Canadian experience, with an accompanying spouse or partner | 140 |
| Qualifying job offer | 100 |

The experience maximum includes existing work-length points. It is therefore misleading to assume every candidate receives 150 new points on top of the previous score. The occupation and duration rules determine what can actually be claimed.

## High wage refers to the occupation

Eligibility uses IRCC’s designated NOC occupation tiers, based on occupational median wages relative to the overall workforce benchmark. A person’s salary alone does not establish that their occupation qualifies. Check the official occupation list rather than inferring eligibility from a pay cheque.

The removal of the LMIA requirement for qualifying Express Entry offers also does not remove the need for a genuine offer or create a general exemption from work-permit requirements.

## French candidates are still part of the ranking

The additional French-language points remain a separate CRS consideration. However, candidates in the French category can also have Canadian experience or qualifying offers. Changes in their scores can affect ranking within that category. It is too early to claim that French cut-offs will be unaffected.

IRCC says recalculation of existing profiles may take up to two weeks, with invitation rounds paused during that process. Check your recalculated score and the official guidance before relying on a previously saved estimate.

For learning decisions, avoid reacting to one predicted threshold. Keep building all four language abilities while reviewing occupation, work history and supporting documents. The reform changes the scoring framework; it does not allow a school to predict an invitation.
