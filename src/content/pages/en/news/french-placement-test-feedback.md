You can read an email in French and understand what the writer needs, yet stall when someone asks you to explain the same thing aloud. Both observations are useful when deciding which class to join. Placement helps identify the class in which you can follow the work and still have something to practise.

*The feedback below is an illustrative report created for this article. The cover is an AI-generated illustration.*

## What the appointment covers

At Global French Education, the free placement test takes 40 minutes. The first 30 minutes cover listening, reading and grammar. The final 10 minutes are a speaking assessment. You can take the test online or in Brampton. The school gives verbal feedback at the appointment and sends written guidance afterwards. See the [enrolment steps](/enrollment) for details.

When you book, mention what you use French for now and what you want to do next. Someone who reads French at work every day may need a different starting point from someone who speaks at home but rarely writes.

## A sample report

**Learner’s goal.** Handle short conversations at work and decide later whether to prepare for a French proficiency exam.

| Area | What the teacher observed | What to practise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading | Followed the main point of a B1-style email and found the requested action. | Summarize the writer’s request in one sentence without copying it. |
| Listening | Understood the purpose of a short message but missed a change in time. | Listen for the phrase that signals the update, then note the new detail. |
| Speaking | Answered familiar questions clearly. When asked for a reason and an example, gave a reason but stopped before developing it. | Give a reason, add one example, then answer a follow-up without restarting. |
| Grammar in use | Recognized the past forms on the worksheet but switched to the present when telling a story aloud. | Retell a short event using two or three time markers. |

**Suggested starting point.** Discuss a B1.1 group and a speaking practice plan. Check after the first two weeks whether the learner can follow the class and extend a spoken answer with one example. Revisit the placement if the group pace makes it hard to participate.

**Next step towards an exam.** Once the learner is comfortable sustaining an exchange, use a timed sample task to get a clearer picture of exam readiness. The timing depends on what that later practice shows.

## Two questions to ask when you get your report

Ask what the teacher heard or saw that led to the recommendation. Then ask what change you should be able to notice first. In this example, the first change could be a 30-second answer that includes one clear reason and one concrete example. That gives the learner something they can record and listen to again after a week.
