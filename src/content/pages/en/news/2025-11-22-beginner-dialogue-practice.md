## Putting familiar expressions to work

This open class put dialogue practice at the centre of the lesson. After reviewing greetings, introductions and basic questions, participants worked in pairs to use that material. The structure made speaking the main task: choose a useful expression, address another person and respond to what comes back.

## Putting the language into practice

The exercises did not require a flawless conversation. They gave learners a setting in which to try language already encountered, including moments when a word was missing or a pause was needed. That experience is useful for identifying the next learning need: a question to review, a sound to practise or a phrase to add.

## A practice idea to take further

Record a short dialogue with a partner. Listen once for whether the exchange makes sense, then choose just one sentence to improve. Repeat the conversation with that small change.

A short exercise is most useful when you return to it. Revisit the exchange on another day, change one detail and notice what you can say without checking your notes. Use any remaining hesitation to choose a question for your next lesson.
