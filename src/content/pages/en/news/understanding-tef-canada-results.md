A TEF Canada practice result is most useful when it tells you what to work on next. A single overall percentage can hide very different levels in listening, reading, speaking and writing.

> **Read the four results separately. For an immigration threshold that applies to every skill, a stronger skill cannot cancel out a weaker one.**

## Know what each section asks

The current TEF Canada format includes reading and listening multiple-choice sections, two writing tasks and two speaking tasks. Writing requires different purposes: continuing a news-style text and developing a supported opinion. Speaking requires active interaction rather than a memorized monologue.

This matters when interpreting feedback. A learner may know grammar well but leave a task incomplete, ask too few relevant follow-up questions or offer reasons that do not answer the situation. Language knowledge and task fulfilment need to develop together.

## Separate practice marks from official reporting

A classroom percentage is not an official TEF score. Do not invent a conversion by multiplying the number of correct answers, or compare a practice rubric directly with another examination’s scale. Official reporting and NCLC correspondence use the test provider’s framework and IRCC’s requirements.

| Feedback area | A useful question |
|---|---|
| Communication | Did I achieve the task’s purpose? |
| Organization | Can the listener or reader follow my ideas? |
| Vocabulary | Are words appropriate and sufficiently precise? |
| Grammar and delivery | Do errors or hesitation interfere with meaning? |

These questions guide preparation; they are not a claim that every criterion has equal official weight.

## Turn the report into a short practice cycle

Choose one weakness that appears repeatedly. For writing, it might be unsupported arguments. For speaking, it might be asking an initial question without responding to the answer. Practise that point in a short new task, obtain feedback and repeat with unfamiliar material.

Compare several performances rather than one favourable score. Consistency across different topics gives a more useful indication of readiness than repeating a familiar sample until the result looks high.
