## Connecting familiar situations

A day off provided the thread for this everyday French class: making plans with a friend, visiting a shop, ordering food and saying goodbye. Linking the situations gave participants a reason to carry language from one activity into another. Greetings, questions and thanks became parts of a connected sequence rather than separate vocabulary exercises.

## Putting the language into practice

Interactive practice encouraged learners to assemble short, natural messages from words they knew. Switching scenes also highlighted the need to understand the purpose of an expression. The same polite opening can be useful in several places, while the request that follows changes with the person and the situation.

## A practice idea to take further

Plan three stops for an imaginary Saturday. Give each stop one question and one response in French. Practise the sequence aloud and notice which expressions you can reuse in all three places.

A short exercise is most useful when you return to it. Revisit the exchange on another day, change one detail and notice what you can say without checking your notes. Use any remaining hesitation to choose a question for your next lesson.
