If you have just moved to Toronto and want to speak French outside class, a conversation circle is a good place to try. You can introduce yourself, ask someone about their neighbourhood and hear what different speakers sound like in the same conversation.

*The cover is an AI-generated illustration of a library setting.*

## Find a session you can join

The Toronto Public Library lists conversation circles on its French programmes page. Open an event listing to check the branch, time, format and registration details. The Centre francophone du Grand Toronto also describes French–English conversation circles on its community activities page. Check whether the session you choose takes place in French or in both languages. [Toronto Public Library French programmes](https://tpl.ca/books-video-music/francais/programmes/) · [Centre francophone du Grand Toronto activities](https://www.centrefranco.org/activit%C3%A9s-rassembleuses)

If you are looking for an outing with a child, check the library’s French programmes for children and look for an age-appropriate storytime. At the branch, *« Bonjour, où se déroule l’heure du conte en français ? »* is a simple way to ask where to go. ## Prepare an introduction and one question

One detail gives the next person something to ask about. Try *« Bonjour, je m’appelle Sam. Je viens d’arriver à Toronto et j’habite à Brampton. Je découvre les bibliothèques du quartier. »* Swap in a detail you are comfortable sharing.

| When you want to… | Try saying… |
| --- | --- |
| Start a conversation | *Vous habitez dans le quartier depuis longtemps ?* |
| Ask for a recommendation | *Vous me conseillez quel endroit pour une première visite ?* |
| Hear something again | *Vous pourriez répéter un peu plus lentement, s’il vous plaît ?* |
| Check what you heard | *Si j’ai bien compris, c’est près de la station ?* |
| Invite another person in | *Et vous, qu’est-ce que vous aimez faire dans le quartier ?* |

A reply gives you your next question. If someone recommends a park, you might ask how to get there, when they usually go, or what they like about it. Pick the follow-up that fits what they actually said.

## Make the first visit manageable

Give yourself one job for your first session. Introduce yourself and ask one follow-up. If the conversation moves faster than you expected, listen for a place, an activity or a time you recognize. Use that detail to take another turn, or ask the speaker to repeat it.

After the circle, write down one phrase you heard and one question you wish you had asked. Say both aloud before your next visit. If you want to prepare with an example, try [answering the same speaking question twice](/en/news/speaking-answer-revision/).
