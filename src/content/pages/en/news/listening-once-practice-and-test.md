Understanding a recording after three plays is different from answering accurately on the first. That difference matters in test preparation, but it does not mean replaying audio is a bad learning habit.

> **Use two modes:** one play to measure first-listen performance; repeated listening to diagnose and learn afterwards.

## Respect the rules of the test you are taking

For TEF Canada and TCF Canada preparation, follow the provider’s instructions for the listening tasks. Do not assume every French examination uses the same playback rules: DELF activities, for example, can have different instructions.

During a simulation, avoid pausing, slowing or replaying the recording beyond what the actual task permits. Otherwise the result measures performance with extra support, which may be useful for learning but is not comparable to the timed task.

## Make the first play informative

Before checking a transcript, write down what you understood. Identify the speaker, situation, key message and any decision or attitude. For a multiple-choice task, note the detail that supports your answer rather than choosing only because a familiar word appears.

If you are unsure, record the uncertainty. An accurate note saying “I missed whether the change was cancelled” is more useful than a confident reconstruction of information you did not hear.

## Then replay with a purpose

Listen again to the difficult section. Compare the sound with the transcript, mark a missed word boundary or a negation, and repeat the short phrase aloud. Close the transcript and return to the recording.

| Stage | Purpose |
|---|---|
| First play | Measure independent understanding. |
| Replay and transcript | Locate the cause of an error. |
| Short spoken repetition | Connect the written expression with its sound. |
| New recording | Check whether the learning transfers. |

The last step matters. Repeated success on one memorized recording is not evidence that every new recording will be easy. Use replay as a learning tool, then test progress with something unfamiliar.
