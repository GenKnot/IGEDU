French-language category rounds are often described as a separate immigration route. A more accurate starting point is that they are a way of selecting eligible candidates within Express Entry.

> **French ability is an additional selection criterion. It does not replace eligibility for an Express Entry program.**

## Start with program eligibility

Before considering a category round, you must qualify for an Express Entry program and enter the pool with an eligible profile. Requirements can concern skilled work, language, education and other factors, depending on the program.

For the French-language proficiency category, IRCC requires French results at NCLC 7 or higher in each of the four abilities. An average is not enough: stronger reading does not compensate for speaking below the required level. The approved French tests for Express Entry are TEF Canada and TCF Canada.

## Eligibility and ranking answer different questions

Eligibility determines whether you can be considered. Your CRS score determines your position among the candidates considered in a particular round. Meeting the category’s language requirement does not remove ranking or guarantee an invitation.

| Stage | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| Program | Am I eligible for an Express Entry program? |
| Language category | Do all four French results meet the requirement? |
| Ranking | How does my current CRS score compare with the relevant round? |
| Application | Can I document everything declared in my profile? |

## Build a plan around the part you can control

Begin with an assessment of your actual language abilities. Someone who reads comfortably may still need substantial work on spontaneous speech or writing under time pressure. A balanced preparation plan protects against one weaker result preventing category eligibility.

At the same time, check the non-language parts of your profile. Learning French cannot fix every issue involving work experience, admissibility or supporting documents. Obtain qualified advice when the application rules are unclear.

Category rounds can create opportunities, but their timing, size and cut-offs remain variable. Use recent results to understand the system, then build your learning plan around demonstrable progress rather than a promised future score.
