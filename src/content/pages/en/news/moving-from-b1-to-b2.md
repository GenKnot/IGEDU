At B1, you may be able to describe your work, explain a familiar experience and manage everyday exchanges. Progress towards B2 asks you to do more with that language: develop ideas, understand viewpoints and respond with greater independence.

> **Keep building vocabulary, but connect it to extended comprehension, interaction and clear organization. No single speaking exercise proves B2 readiness.**

## Develop an idea beyond the first sentence

Take a familiar question such as whether remote work suits every job. State a position, give a reason, illustrate it and acknowledge a limitation. The goal is not to produce an elaborate speech. It is to make your reasoning easy to follow.

Compare “Remote work is good” with “It can reduce commuting, but new employees may need more direct support.” The second sentence adds a condition and a trade-off without requiring obscure vocabulary.

## Listen for the relationship between ideas

Understanding a topic is different from understanding a speaker’s position. During a short interview, note whether the speaker supports a proposal, qualifies it or objects to part of it. Then identify the phrase that supports your interpretation.

Replay with a transcript when needed. The learning task is to connect your interpretation to evidence, not to prove that you never need help.

| Practice | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|
| Speaking | Reasons and examples connect to the main point. |
| Listening | The speaker’s position is supported by a specific clue. |
| Writing | Paragraphs develop the question instead of repeating it. |
| Interaction | You respond to what was actually said. |

## Review more than fluency

A fast answer can remain vague, while a thoughtful answer with a short pause can communicate precisely. Look at range, accuracy, coherence and interaction together. Vocabulary gaps still matter when they repeatedly prevent you from explaining the distinction you intend.

Choose one short topic each week and work on it through listening, discussion and writing. Return later with a new question. Transfer to an unfamiliar task is stronger evidence of progress than polishing one response indefinitely.
