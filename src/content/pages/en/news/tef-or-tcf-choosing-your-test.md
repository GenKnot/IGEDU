One learner finds TEF Canada more comfortable; another prefers TCF Canada. Neither experience establishes that one examination is universally easier. The useful question is which format lets you demonstrate your abilities most consistently.

> **For Express Entry, both are approved French tests. Compare the tasks, then prepare for the exact version you book.**

## Start with your destination

“TEF” and “TCF” describe families of tests. Before comparing preparation materials, confirm that the Canada version meets the requirements of your intended application. A familiar acronym is not enough to establish that a particular certificate will be accepted.

Next, check current availability, fees and practical arrangements directly with an authorized centre. A convenient date is helpful, but it should not become a reason to sit an examination before your weaker skills are ready.

## Compare the work you must produce

| Feature | TEF Canada | TCF Canada |
|---|---|---|
| Speaking | Two interaction tasks | Three tasks, including an interview, interaction and a viewpoint |
| Writing | Two tasks | Three tasks |
| Preparation focus | Sustaining questions and persuasion; adapting to each writing purpose | Switching between task types; managing each task’s length and timing |

These differences concern task demands, not the value of the resulting language evidence. Do not assume that fewer tasks mean less difficulty or that a longer section always gives more time per question.

## Try a fair comparison

Complete an unfamiliar sample from each test under its actual instructions. Record speaking, respect writing limits and avoid pausing the listening recording. Afterwards, compare where the difficulty appeared: understanding the task, finding ideas, organizing language or managing time.

Choose on the basis of that evidence. Once you decide, stay with one format long enough to build reliable habits. Frequently switching tests after a disappointing practice result can consume time without addressing the underlying language issue.

Good preparation develops French first, then teaches you to use it within the chosen examination. A format decision should support that work, not replace it.
