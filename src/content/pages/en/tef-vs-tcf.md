## The short answer

**Both exams are accepted by Canadian immigration and carry exactly the same weight. You lose nothing by choosing either one.**

There are only two real differences:

1. **Pace:** the TCF has more questions and tighter timing; the TEF is more relaxed.
2. **Speaking:** the TEF speaking test is 15 minutes in two parts; the TCF is 12 minutes in three parts, one of which is an unprepared, improvised talk.

Which one to choose depends on what worries you more: running out of time, or having to speak without preparation.

## The basics

| | TEF Canada | TCF Canada |
|---|---|---|
| Run by | CCI Paris Île-de-France | France Éducation international |
| Accepted for immigration | Yes | Yes |
| Validity | 2 years | 2 years |
| Required sections | Listening, reading, writing, speaking: all four | Listening, reading, writing, speaking: all four |
| Grammar section | No | No |
| Results used for | Immigration, some institutions | Immigration, some institutions |

**Note:** for immigration you need **TEF Canada**, not plain TEF, and **TCF Canada**, not TCF tout public. One word apart, completely different uses.

## Structure compared

| Section | TEF Canada | TCF Canada |
|---|---|---|
| Listening (CO) | About 40 minutes | About 35 minutes |
| Reading (CE) | About 60 minutes | About 60 minutes |
| Writing (EE) | About 60 minutes, 2 tasks | About 60 minutes, 3 tasks |
| Speaking (EO) | About 15 minutes, 2 parts | About 12 minutes, 3 parts |

> Exact durations are those published by the exam provider; check them before you register.

## Where the real difference lies

### Writing

**TEF:** two tasks. The first is a short factual text (organizing information clearly); the second is an argument (taking a position and persuading). You have time to think, and time to revise.

**TCF:** three tasks. The first is a message to a friend, the second a narration or description, the third an argument. More tasks means **less time for each**, so there is little room to revise repeatedly.

**In practice:** if you write slowly, the TEF is more comfortable. If you think fast and get it right in one go, the TCF will not hurt you.

### Speaking

**TEF:** two parts. In the first you obtain information (you have to ask the questions); in the second you persuade (you have to bring the examiner round to your view). There is preparation time between them.

**TCF:** three parts. The first is a guided conversation, the second an interactive task, the third an **unprepared, improvised statement of opinion**.

**In practice:** if improvising worries you, the third part of the TCF is the hardest. If having to ask questions on your own initiative worries you, the first part of the TEF is more awkward.

### Listening

In both exams the recording plays **only once**.

TEF material is more situational (conversations, messages, announcements); TCF material is shorter and faster, with more questions. In both, two seconds of distraction and you lose the thread.

## How to choose: three questions

**Question 1: do you write fast?**
Not really → TEF. The TCF leaves little time for its three writing tasks.

**Question 2: can you speak for 2 minutes with no preparation?**
No → TEF. The TCF includes an improvised talk.

**Question 3: in mock exams, which one do you do better on?**
This is the most practical test. At the same level, take a mock exam of each and compare your scores. **Do not go by feeling; go by scores.**

## Four common mistakes

**1. Thinking one exam can stand in for the other**
It cannot. The TEF and TCF are two separate results with no conversion between them. Immigration accepts both, but you submit only one.

**2. Thinking you can take just one section**
TEF Canada and TCF Canada both require all four sections. Only TEFAQ and TCFQ (the Quebec versions) allow you to take just listening and speaking.

**3. Miscounting validity**
IRCC requires results to be less than 2 years old **when you create your Express Entry profile and again when you submit your permanent residence application**. Check the date printed on your result. Some people book too close to the limit and find their result already expired at submission.

**4. Registering for the wrong version**
"TEF" and "TEF Canada" are two different exams. If you pick the wrong one on the registration page, the result cannot be used for immigration.

## Differences in preparation

| | TEF preparation | TCF preparation |
|---|---|---|
| Core skill | Organization and depth of expression | Reaction speed and stamina for a large number of questions |
| Writing | Structure and levels of argument | Writing quickly, revising little |
| Speaking | Questioning technique, persuasive structure | Organizing ideas on the spot, giving an opinion in a short time |
| Mock exam pacing | One full paper, no interruption | Practice with compressed time, to get used to turning the page unfinished |
